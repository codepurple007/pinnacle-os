# Case Scenario Expansion — The Agency Founder

**Subject:** Alex Quinn, Founder & Managing Director, Pinnacle Marketing  
**Document type:** Operating case (narrative + evidence)  
**Feeds:** SRS v1.0 (`docs/02-srs-pinnacle-os.md`)  
**Phase:** 1 of 8

This document expands the brief into a world a product can be designed against. Names, clients, and numbers are representative composites drawn from documented agency-founder patterns (inbox-as-operating-system, 15-hour-class admin weeks, reactive calendars, client happiness vs. delivery lag). They are specific on purpose. Vague “busy founder” stories produce chatbots. This story must produce a gatekeeper.

---

## 1. Who Alex is, and why the agency looks fine from the outside

Alex Quinn is 38, based in Brooklyn, and founded Pinnacle Marketing in 2019 after six years as a brand strategist at a mid-size holding-company shop. Pinnacle is a boutique performance-and-brand hybrid: retainers that mix paid social, landing-page creative, and monthly reporting. It is not a freelancer disguised as an agency. There is a real team, real payroll, and real clients who treat Alex as the account lead even when they were introduced to Morgan, the project manager.

On paper the business is healthy. Annual run-rate sits around $1.1M. Gross margin after freelancer costs is roughly 48%. There are 13 active retainers and a waitlist Alex never has time to convert. The agency is profitable every month. There is no cash crisis. There is a **capacity crisis that looks like a communication success**.

Alex is the product the clients bought. They hired “Alex’s taste” and then inherited an inbox. New business still comes from referrals to Alex personally. When a CMO forwards a thread, it is Alex’s name in the To: field, not `team@`. Alex has tried to route work through Morgan. Clients cc Alex anyway. Alex replies anyway, because a two-hour delay on a $12k/month retainer feels like malpractice, and because Alex still believes that responsiveness is the brand.

That belief is the trap.

## 2. The company as it actually operates

### 2.1 Headcount

| Person | Role | What they actually do | How they collide with Alex’s inbox |
| ------ | ---- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Alex Quinn** | Founder | Sales, strategy, final creative taste, every “can we hop on a call,” payroll sign-off, freelancer rates | Switchboard. Nothing leaves the building that Alex has not at least skimmed. |
| **Jordan Hale** | Designer (FTE) | Paid social units, landing pages, brand kits | Receives work late because the request sat in Alex’s inbox for 11 hours. Clients sometimes email Jordan directly; Jordan forwards to Alex “to keep you in the loop,” which recreates the pile. |
| **Sam Okonkwo** | Copywriter (FTE) | Ads, landing copy, email sequences | Cannot start until Alex answers the client’s “we’re not sure about the headline” email that is actually a yes/no. |
| **Riley Chen** | Ads Manager (FTE) | Meta + Google spend, weekly dashboards, pause/unpause | Riley already sent the report. Clients still email Alex “what did we spend?” because they do not open the dashboard. |
| **Morgan Patel** | Project Manager (FTE) | Timelines, freelancer coordination, ClickUp, client status decks | Morgan is hired to be the traffic controller. Clients bypass Morgan. Alex undermines Morgan by answering first. |
| **Rotating 5–10 freelancers** | Video, extra design, web builds, SEO spikes, photography | Burst capacity | Availability questions land on Alex at 7:10 AM because the freelancer Slack-DMs and emails at once, and Alex is the only one who knows the rate and the client’s NDA.

There is no executive assistant. There was a part-time VA for four months in 2025. The VA could label mail and send Calendly links. The VA could not tell a dashboard question from a strategy meeting, could not read campaign context, and once confirmed a call that double-booked a new-business pitch. Alex fired the process, not the person, and took the inbox back “just until we hire.” They never hired.

### 2.2 Clients (the 13 retainers that create the noise)

These are the accounts that generate 80% of inbound mail. They are not all difficult. They are **asymmetric**: two of them produce half the interruptions.

| Client | Monthly retainer | Primary contact | Pattern |
| ------ | ---------------- | --------------- | ------- |
| **Northline Athletics** | $14,500 | Dana Voss, CMO | Fast, fair, expects 15-minute replies. Sends “quick questions” that are actually report lookups. Happiest on Slack energy, but insists on email for “paper trail.” |
| **Harbor & Pine (DTC home)** | $12,000 | Ellis Ward, Founder | Anxious. Interprets silence as failure. Will request a call for anything that could be a sentence. Currently unhappy with *speed of deliverables* while praising “how easy Alex is to reach.” |
| **Kite & Co. (fintech)** | $11,000 | Priya Raman, Head of Growth | Precise, hates meetings. Gets angry if a call is booked for something a spreadsheet answers. |
| **Solstice Clinics** (healthcare adjacent) | $9,800 | Marcus Bell | Compliance-sensitive. Wrong auto-reply here is a legal problem, not an awkward one. |
| **Bramble Coffee** | $8,200 | Owner-operator | Informal, charming. Expects Alex’s voice, not “as per my last email.” |
| **Eight more retainers** | $4k–$7k | Mixed | Lower drama, same inbox tax: scheduling, “did you get this?”, freelancer questions, newsletter CCs. |

Alex wants **20 retainers**. At current operating style, client 14 is a threat, not a win. Every new logo adds another human who believes they have Alex’s mobile attention.

### 2.3 Tool stack (the island chain Alex swims between)

- **Gmail** — the real operating system. Labels exist (`Clients`, `Freelancers`, `Invoices`) and are ignored. Starred mail is a graveyard of good intentions.
- **Google Calendar** — public-facing truth. No buffers. No meeting types. If a slot is empty, it is available. Lunch is optional.
- **Slack** — `#delivery`, `#ads`, DMs from Riley when spend spikes. Alex has Slack on the phone. Email still wins because clients are not in the workspace.
- **HubSpot** — contacts and deal stages. Last-touch dates are stale because Alex does not log calls.
- **Notion** — client FAQs, brand voice, “where is the latest report” links. Search is slow; Alex remembers instead of querying.
- **ClickUp** — Morgan’s board. Client requests in email are not tickets until Morgan copies them by hand.
- **Meta Ads Manager / Google Ads** — Riley’s world. Clients email Alex for numbers that already live in the weekly PDF attached to last Thursday’s send.
- **Calendly** — installed, linked in the footer, used as a last resort. Clients ignore it and write “are you free Thursday?” because that is how they have always booked Alex.

The AI Chief of Staff does not need a new tool for Alex to live in. It needs to **live where the work already arrives**: the inbox, then the calendar, with Slack as the alarm bell.

## 3. The “living inside the inbox” loop — a reconstructed Tuesday

The following is one representative day, not a caricature. Time-tracking (RescueTime + a manual log Morgan asked Alex to keep for two weeks) averaged **3.3 hours/day Mon–Fri on email admin**, which is **16.5 hours/week**, rounding in the brief to the 18-hour class when weeks include freelancer fires and month-end reporting. Tuesday 18 March is the day used for design scenarios.

### 3.1 7:00 AM — “just a quick check”

Alex opens Gmail on a laptop at the kitchen counter. There are 41 unread since 6:00 PM yesterday. Three are actually from this morning.

**Email A — 6:41 AM, Dana Voss (Northline)**  
Subject: Re: Weekly recap — week of 10 Mar  
> Quick one before my standup — what did we spend on Facebook last month? I have a board packet at 9. The recap PDF is huge and I can’t find the number.

The PDF *is* attached to this same thread, page 2, “Paid social — February.” Riley sent it Thursday. Dana did not open it. Alex knows the number is $27,440 because Riley Slack’d it yesterday. Alex still opens Ads Manager “to be sure,” types a four-line reply, and hits send at 7:12. Elapsed: 11 minutes. Nature of work: **lookup that should not require the founder**.

**Email B — 7:04 AM, freelancer Maya (motion)**  
Subject: Thu shoot — Northline still on?  
> Hi Alex, confirming I’m holding Thursday 2–6. Need a yes/no by 9 or I’ll take another booking. Also, invoice from last month still outstanding?

Alex knows Thursday is a Harbor & Pine check-in at 2:30 that was booked last night. Alex has not told Maya. Alex replies “let me confirm and come back,” which is a promise to re-enter the pile. Elapsed: 6 minutes. Nature of work: **availability + AP, two systems, one human**.

**Email C — 7:18 AM, Ellis Ward (Harbor & Pine)**  
Subject: Can we move tomorrow?  
> Hey — I need to move tomorrow’s 11 AM to Thursday. Morning is better if you can. Also still waiting on the PDP hero set — we were supposed to have options Monday.

This is two intents in one email: reschedule, and a delivery miss. Alex treats the calendar first because it is mechanical and therefore soothing. Tomorrow 11 AM is deleted. Thursday morning is already a Kite & Co. working session. Alex offers “Thursday 2 PM?” without checking Maya’s hold or the Harbor delivery status. Ellis replies “2 works” at 7:31. The PDP delay is not addressed. Elapsed: 14 minutes. Nature of work: **reactive calendar + ignored delivery signal**.

By **7:45 AM** Alex has rescheduled a client call, half-answered a freelancer, and answered a report question whose answer was already in the client’s weekly email. The brief’s loop is not a metaphor. It is the first 45 minutes, every weekday.

### 3.2 8:00 AM–12:00 PM — the context-switching tax

Alex blocks 8:00–10:00 as “proposal time”: a new service SKU (always-on creative retainer) that would raise average revenue per client. The block is a fiction.

- 8:07 — Slack from Riley: Northline CPA spiked overnight. Alex writes “look at it after lunch.”
- 8:19 — Email from Kite & Co.: “Do we need a call to walk through the Q3 brief or can you just send the doc?” Alex, still in founder-hero mode, replies “happy to jump on a call, here is my Calendly.” Priya wanted the doc. A 45-minute meeting is now on Thursday. Alex has trained the wrong behavior.
- 8:41 — Email from a prospect referred by Bramble. Alex stars it. Stars are not a CRM.
- 9:02 — Morgan pings: Harbor is waiting on copy from Sam; Sam is waiting on Alex’s email about the headline. The email is in Drafts from last night.
- 9:15–9:50 — Alex answers six “quick” mails in a batch, feels productive, and returns to the proposal. The proposal’s opening paragraph is rewritten for the third time because the thread of argument is gone.

The 15-minute refocus number in the brief is conservative for knowledge work. Twenty “checks” a day do not cost 20 × 2 minutes of typing. They cost **20 fractures**. Even if only eight of them fully break a deep-work block, that is two hours of proposal-quality thought that never happens. Combined with the 3.3 hours of direct email admin, Alex’s Tuesday has almost no continuous hour in which a new offer can be written or Riley’s creative brief can be reviewed with taste instead of skimmed between pings.

By noon Alex has attended two calls that should have been emails (a Northline “where is the file” that Morgan could have answered, and an internal standup that Alex joined late and derailed). The calendar is full. The agency is not moving.

### 3.3 1:00 PM — the happiness paradox, named

Ellis (Harbor & Pine) replies again:

> You’re the most responsive agency we’ve ever used, which I love. I’m just worried we’re slipping on output. We still don’t have the hero options. If this is a capacity issue I need to know. Happy to hop on a call today or tomorrow.

This is the paradox in a single paragraph. **Communication SLA: excellent. Delivery SLA: failing.** Ellis is not wrong. The hero set is late because Jordan started Thursday instead of Tuesday, because the request sat in Alex’s personal inbox under a thread titled “Re: catch-up,” because Alex answered the reschedule and not the asset, because Morgan did not see the request until Alex forwarded it at 11:40.

Clients learn that emailing Alex works. They do not learn that ClickUp works. The incentive system is inverted: the founder who answers fastest gets more questions, fewer uninterrupted hours, slower delivery, then more “why is this late?” emails.

If Alex hires more delivery people without changing the switchboard, the inbox gets worse. More doers create more status questions that still route to Alex.

### 3.4 2:00–5:00 PM — the blind calendar

A new client, Solstice, asks: “Can we talk through the landing page comments?” Alex books a 30-minute default slot. The comments are three bullets, all copy nits Sam could have handled in writing. The meeting still happens because there is no classifier, no rule that says **question-that-can-be-answered-in-text must not become a meeting**.

Meanwhile Northline asks about Q3 planning. That *is* a 45-minute strategy review. It gets the same 30-minute leftover on Friday afternoon when Alex is already tired. Face-to-face quality degrades because every email is treated with the same urgency and the same meeting template. Strategic work is squeezed into leftover inventory. Status theater consumes prime hours.

Alex is not undisciplined. Alex is **untriaged**. The calendar is a suggestion box with a send button.

### 3.5 5:12 PM — the breaking point

Alex is still in Gmail. Morgan has gone to pick up a child. Riley’s weekly report for Bramble is due. Alex has not opened the always-on creative proposal since 8:41 AM.

A HubSpot notification: the Bramble referral prospect booked nothing, because the starred email was never answered with a time, a packet, or a no. That logo would have been client 14.

Alex writes in a private Notion page, later copied into this case:

> We cannot get to 20 clients if I am the switchboard operator. Calendly did not save me. A VA could not tell a dashboard question from a strategy fight. I need something that lives *inside* the inbox — that reads the thread, knows the client, books the right kind of time or refuses the meeting, and only wakes me when someone is actually angry or the strategy is on the table.

That is the product request. Not a chatbot. Not a scheduling link. An **AI Chief of Staff** whose office is Gmail.

## 4. The toll, stated so it can be measured

### 4.1 Direct time (the 18-hour drain)

Two-week time study (Alex + RescueTime categories “Email” and “Calendar” only):

| Metric | Value |
| ------ | ----- |
| Email admin per weekday | 3.3 hours |
| Weekdays | 5 |
| Weekly email-admin load | 16.5 hours |
| Peak weeks (month-end reports + freelancer AP) | ~18 hours |
| Share of a 45-hour founder week | 37–40% |
| Email checks per day (phone + desktop) | ~20 |
| Client-facing SLA Alex currently hits | under 15 minutes during 8 AM–7 PM |

This is not “Alex should type faster.” The work is classification, retrieval, coordination, and emotional labor. Those are assistant jobs.

### 4.2 Context-switching tax

If even half of the 20 daily checks interrupt a focus block, and recovery is ~15 minutes, the hidden bill is on the order of **2.5 hours of lost deep work** on a clean day and **5–6 hours** on a day like Tuesday, when checks cluster inside the proposal block. The proposal SKU, the thing that would raise revenue without adding 7 more retainers of the same chaos, does not exist because the inbox does.

### 4.3 Quality tax on meetings

Default 30-minute bookings for every “can we talk” produce:

- under-prepared strategy sessions
- over-meeting’d clients who wanted a number
- no buffers, so every call starts late and Alex is the person who is always catching up

### 4.4 Organizational tax

Morgan cannot own traffic if Alex keeps being faster than process. Jordan and Sam idle. Riley answers the same spend question twice: once in the PDF, once via Alex. Freelancers get late yes/no and take other bookings. The agency’s ceiling is not talent. It is **Alex’s attention as a shared mutex**.

### 4.5 What “90% admin reduction” means in this case

The SRS target of a 90% cut in founder administrative workload is the north star, not a day-one guarantee. For this case, success is operationally:

- Alex’s weekday email-admin time moves from ~3.3 hours toward **~20–30 minutes** of review (drafts, escalations, digest).
- Sub-15-minute client response **still happens**, but the hands on the keyboard are the agent’s, with Alex’s voice, except when sentiment or strategy requires a human.
- Harbor-class delivery lag stops being caused by requests trapped in the founder inbox.
- Meetings exist only when the thread says they must.

## 5. Why existing “solutions” already failed (so the product does not repeat them)

**Calendly / a scheduling link.** Shifts typing onto the client. Does not classify intent. Does not stop a meeting that should be an email. Does not offer *three specific times with context* (“Q3 planning — 45 minutes — here are Thursday 10:00, Friday 9:30, Monday 2:00”). Clients who already have Alex’s ear will not self-serve.

**ChatGPT / Gemini in a browser.** Alex still copy-pastes. No standing connection to Gmail or Calendar. No confidence score. No audit trail. No undo. Hallucinates spend. Cannot be trusted with Solstice.

**A human VA without systems.** Cheap for labels, expensive for judgment. No CRM injection. Double-booking risk. Clients detect the voice shift. Alex re-checks every draft, which is the same 3.3 hours with extra latency.

**Hiring another PM.** Helps delivery if and only if inbound is redirected. Without a gatekeeper on Alex’s actual inbox, the new PM becomes another person waiting on Alex.

**Pausing new clients.** Protects quality, kills the 20-client goal. Not a strategy; a surrender.

The missing artifact is an agent that is **wrong in inspectable ways** (drafts, scores, logs, undo) rather than **silent in heroic ways** (Alex answering everything personally).

## 6. What the AI Chief of Staff must do on *this* Tuesday

Mapped to the morning pile, not to abstract features.

| Real event | Wrong product behavior | Required behavior | SRS hook |
| ---------- | ---------------------- | ----------------- | -------- |
| Dana: “What did we spend on Facebook last month?” | Book a call; or guess $25k | Retrieve from report/KB (and later Meta). Draft a short factual reply. Confidence high → pending approval or send per policy. No meeting. | FR-01, FR-02, FR-06, FR-07, UC-01 |
| Maya: Thursday hold + unpaid invoice | Founder ping-pong | Availability is a calendar + freelancer lane. AP is finance — escalate to Alex or bookkeeper, do not freelance a legal promise. Scheduling lane separate from invoice. | FR-01, FR-09, FR-10 |
| Ellis: move 11 AM to Thursday + late PDP | Reschedule only; miss the delivery anger | Reschedule without double-booking (Kite already has Thursday morning; Maya hold; Harbor 2:30). Surface delivery miss as possible negative sentiment / urgent delivery. Slack Alex on creative delay language. | FR-03, FR-05, FR-09, UC-02 |
| Priya: “call or just send the doc?” | Instant Calendly | Classify as text-capable. Send the Q3 doc (or draft). Do not book. | FR-06 |
| Ellis: “unhappy with speed / hop on a call” | Empathy paragraph from the model, auto-sent | Negative sentiment → **no auto-respond**. Slack: Harbor is unhappy about delivery. Pending Human. | FR-03, FR-09 |
| Solstice: “talk through comments” | 30-min default | Comments are copy nits → written reply, Sam/Morgan notified, no meeting. | FR-06, team notify |
| Dana: “Q3 planning” | 30-min leftover Friday | 45-min Deep Dive, three dated options in prime hours, context in the invite. | FR-04, FR-06 |
| 5:00 PM | Alex still triaging | Daily Executive Summary: autonomous count, approval queue, tomorrow’s top 3. | FR-10 |

Voice constraint: Bramble and Harbor must not receive the same corporate template. Tone profiles are not decoration; they are how the agency avoids the “this is an AI” tell (NFR-06).

Safety constraint: Solstice and any healthcare-adjacent claim must sit behind higher review. Auto-send allow-lists default **off** in MVP (SRS). Alex trusts, then verifies.

## 7. Email corpus (design fixtures)

These are the minimum golden examples Phase 4–5 must classify. They should be seeded in the demo (Phase 8).

### 7.1 UC-01 — lookup, not a meeting

**From:** Dana Voss `<dana@northline.co>`  
**Subject:** Re: Weekly recap — week of 10 Mar  
**Body:** Quick one before my standup — what did we spend on Facebook last month? I have a board packet at 9. The recap PDF is huge and I can’t find the number.

**Expected category:** General Client Question  
**Sentiment:** Neutral  
**Meeting:** No  
**Retrieval:** February paid-social total from weekly report / KB  
**Draft sketch:** One paragraph, exact figure, pointer to PDF page, offer to send the board-ready snippet.  
**Auto-send:** Only if confidence ≥ 85% **and** client is on allow-list; else Drafts.

### 7.2 UC-02 — angry creative / delivery

**From:** Ellis Ward `<ellis@harborandpine.com>`  
**Subject:** Creative quality  
**Body:** I am unhappy with the creative quality on the last round. The PDP heroes feel generic and not like us. Let’s jump on a call about this today.

**Expected category:** Urgent Delivery Issue  
**Sentiment:** Negative  
**Auto-respond:** Forbidden  
**Action:** Urgent Escalation, Slack ping, Pending Human, summary for Alex  
**Human-only phrase:** “Let’s jump on a call about this”

### 7.3 Smart reschedule

**From:** Ellis Ward  
**Subject:** Can we move tomorrow?  
**Body:** I need to move tomorrow’s 11 AM to Thursday.

**Expected category:** Scheduling/Rescheduling  
**Action:** Cancel tomorrow 11:00, scan Thursday, first valid 30-minute slot that does not collide (founder hours, buffers, existing Kite block), send invite modification with human note.  
**Forbidden:** Double-book, silent drop of the delivery complaint if present in the same thread.

### 7.4 Meeting-type: strategy vs status vs refuse

| Snippet | Type | Booking |
| ------- | ---- | ------- |
| “Can we do Q3 planning next week?” | Strategy Review | 45-min Deep Dive, three options |
| “5-minute status on the landing page?” | Status Update | 15-min Stand-up |
| “How do I download last week’s report from the dashboard?” | FAQ / text | No booking; written steps from KB |
| “Can you explain the logic behind this strategy?” | Human-only | No auto strategy essay; Pending Human (FR-09) |

### 7.5 UC-03 — founder asks the agent

**From:** Alex (to agent address or labeled command)  
**Body:** What’s my schedule like today?

**Action:** Calendar parse, bulleted summary, link to full calendar. This is the founder’s “trust but verify” surface besides the digest.

### 7.6 Spam / newsletter

Industry newsletters, tool receipts, LinkedIn. **Auto-archive.** Must not Slack. Must not draft.

### 7.7 Freelancer availability

Maya’s Thursday hold. **Category:** Freelancer/Team Availability. Notify Morgan on Slack/email assignment path. Do not let Alex be the only clock.

## 8. People the system must not surprise

**The Founder (primary).** Non-technical, accuracy-obsessed, will disable the product after one wrong send to a paying client. Needs: drafts, confidence, Slack for fires, undo within 5 minutes, a 5 PM digest that makes Gmail optional after hours.

**The Team (secondary).** Jordan, Sam, Riley, Morgan should receive “work has arrived” without waiting for Alex to forward. MVP can notify via Slack/email. Auto-creating ClickUp cards is **post-MVP**.

**The Client (external).** Should feel Alex: fast, specific, warm enough for Bramble, precise enough for Kite. They should not be told they are talking to an AI unless the context is scheduling logistics. Empathy is required; confession is not.

## 9. Success picture (so Phase 8 can film it)

A demo Tuesday should show this sequence without a slide deck:

1. Dana’s spend question is drafted with the real figure in under a minute; Alex taps approve on a phone; no meeting appears.
2. Ellis’s quality complaint never generates a soothing auto-reply; Alex’s Slack lights up with a six-line brief.
3. “Move 11 AM to Thursday” becomes one clean invite on a free slot; Kite’s session is untouched.
4. A “how do I download the report” email never books 30 minutes.
5. At 5:00 PM Alex receives: 14 handled, 3 awaiting approval, tomorrow’s top 3 (including a 45-minute Q3 deep dive that was booked as a deep dive, not a leftover).
6. Alex spends the recovered morning block on the proposal that currently does not exist.

If those six shots work, the case is proven. If the product can chat wittily but still books a call for Dana’s spend question, it has failed this case — no matter how good the model sounds.

## 10. Phase 1 close-out

This case is detailed enough to design against. The bottleneck is not intelligence. It is **Alex functioning as a synchronous API in front of a team that already has reports, boards, and a PM**. PinnacleOS exists to take that API off the founder, keep the 15-minute communication promise, and give the hours back to strategy, taste, and delivery — the work only Alex can do, and the work the 20-client goal actually requires.

Traceability into requirements lives in the SRS, §8.
